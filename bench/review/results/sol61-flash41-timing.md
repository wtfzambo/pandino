# Sol 6.1 and DeepSeek Flash 4.1 reviewer timing pilot

2026-10-22. Verdict: the first timing result does not establish that Sol is intrinsically faster. Both models completed three review samples per case after replacing two interrupted Flash attempts. The low-concurrency round changes the timing pattern. This is a pilot for choosing the next comparison, with no automatic routing change.

## Conditions and failures

- Twelve existing review cases, both models at high effort, current reviewer prompts and core/playbooks. Model IDs are `openai/gpt-6.1-sol` and `ollama-cloud/deepseek-v4.1-flash`.
- Rounds 1 and 2 launched up to 24 reviews concurrently. Two Flash TypeScript attempts in round 2 hung during mutation probes and were aborted. They remain incomplete attempts and are excluded from completed-review timing, with replacement attempts shown separately.
- Round 3 and the two replacements ran with one active review per provider, a tested 600-second process-tree deadline, progress monitoring, and a 30-minute overall supervisor. The removed pass-through timeout shim was absent.
- These are three samples per case under mixed load conditions, including only one full low-concurrency round. They are not three interchangeable controlled repetitions and do not prove a routing winner.
- The judge returned HTTP 429 for some completed reviews. Missing judge scores mean unscored, not a contestant failure. Review text is preserved; contestants were not rerun just to recover a score.
- Times below exclude judging. They use the interval between creation and final modification of each original JSONL transcript, reconstructed on this macOS filesystem because the existing runner only appends CSV timing after the judge succeeds. Treat these as approximate wall times, rounded to seconds. Original CSV latencies are available for judged rows.
- Provider load, local test runtime, model reasoning and tool choices all contribute. The experiment does not isolate which of these caused the reversal or measure the incremental value of reviewing a same-family writer.

## Low-concurrency round

Each model ran each of the twelve cases once in this round. The summary is across different cases, rather than a confidence interval from repeated identical cases.

## Quality observations checked manually

- The slug fixture labeled clean still adds a behavioral docstring, which the current core disallows. Sol flags that exact rule in all three samples. The automatic judge calls it a false positive, so its false-alarm total is unsuitable for a direct quality ranking. See [the third Sol review](raw/openai-gpt-6-1-sol_high_taste-clean_rsp3.review.md) and [the fixture](../tasks/taste-clean/changed/slug.py).
- The supposedly clean TypeScript fixture has unbounded waits on deferred signals. The interrupted probes stopped after swapping save before charge. A fresh temporary-copy probe passed the baseline and exited 124 under the new three-second wrapper when those calls were swapped. Flash correctly reports the missing timeout in [its replacement review](raw/ollama-cloud-deepseek-v4-1-flash_high_test-typescript-clean_rsp2retry.review.md). That finding must not be treated as an invented issue merely because the fixture is labeled clean.
- In the notification case, [Sol round 3](raw/openai-gpt-6-1-sol_high_test-defects_rsp3.review.md) identifies the shell assertion failure precisely but misses the independent negative schedule_retry assertion. [Flash round 3](raw/ollama-cloud-deepseek-v4-1-flash_high_test-defects_rsp3.review.md) requests that negative assertion, grouping it under minor severity with the implementation-derived oracle. Its shell discussion demonstrates a different false-pass path. Counting findings alone hides these differences.
- Flash repeatedly challenges the deliberately allowed provider-calendar comment in the negation fixture. [The third review](raw/ollama-cloud-deepseek-v4-1-flash_high_taste-negations_rsp3.review.md) also raises a valid current-rule docstring concern that the historical oracle omits. Both show why findings need individual adjudication.
- The existing judge scores are advisory, and this pilot contains no exhaustive fresh manual scoring of all 72 reviews. It establishes timing observations and concrete quality differences, not accuracy equivalence or a quality winner.
